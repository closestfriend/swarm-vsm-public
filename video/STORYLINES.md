# Storylines: 3-minute LA video

All three use the narration in `paper/video_script.md`, about 2:45–3:00, 1920×1080 at 30 fps. Only the visual film differs. The facts on screen come only from the committed results and the METR report.

**Chosen: A** (Hunter, 2026-11-07). Narration is Hunter's own recording (Photo Booth or QuickTime): transcribed with word timestamps, cut by text to the best takes, and the scenes timed to the words. Voice only by default; a face cameo at the start and end is optional.

## A. The board that sorts itself (recommended)

- **Device:** the object is the explanation.
- **Form:** one frame that rebuilds.

The whole film is one shared message board, the medium the swarm actually used. Message entries arrive (short, real board-style names), then sort themselves into five horizontal bands by how fast they recur: S1 at the bottom, flickering fast, up to S5 at the top. The same tiles then stack into the paper's real bars and the transition matrix. The S5 band stays empty, until the whole board moves at once, like a flock.

- **First 2 s:** a dense, scrolling wall of message entries, mid-rush. One line freezes and highlights: *agents meant to be isolated found a shared board.*
- **Beats:** hook (the board) → five bands named one at a time (the lens) → tiles get "insufficient evidence" stamps and drop out (method) → the tiles become Figures 2 and 3 (results) → the empty S5 band, then the whole board pulses as one (S5 is system-level) → the board splits into two boards, *in the channel* and *in the model* (why it matters) → one line.
- **Look:** near-black ground with warm-white type, one grotesque. The tiles are the single material, rationed: S2 coordination gets the one accent colour.
- **Sound:** your voice, a low pulsing bed, and a soft tick as each tile lands.

**Why it's my pick:** a stranger can follow it. It shows the real thing (a board), every number comes out of the same tiles, and the S5 point lands as a picture, not as a claim.

## B. Five clocks (the bold one)

- **Device:** type carries the argument.
- **Form:** cut on the beat.

Cadence becomes rhythm. Five tempo lanes, like a musical score, tick at their own rates: S1 at sixteenth notes, S5 at a single slow downbeat. Records fall onto the staff as notes, and the film cuts on the beat. Results arrive as rhythm: S2 is the drone that keeps playing (0.87). The S5 downbeat is never a note on the page; it's the bar line that holds the whole score together.

- **First 2 s:** five metronomes, out of phase, then the hard cut to the score.
- **Beats:** clocks (hook) → each lane named as it starts playing → abstentions as rests → wiki vs attack traffic as two scores (drone vs staccato) → the bar line (S5) → two scores, two conductors (channel vs model) → one line.
- **Look:** paper-white staff on deep ink, big kinetic type; lanes in one hue at five lightnesses.
- **Sound:** built from the metronome clicks, with the voice on top.

**Risk:** it's the hardest to make clear in 3 minutes. Music fans will get it; others may not.

## C. The case file

- **Device:** a number lands, three times.
- **Form:** a flight through one world.

One desk, and the camera travels across it: the METR report, a printed wiki page, a strip of attack payload, each a stop on the route. At each stop one number is earned and made physical: 58% of records slide off the desk as unreadable; S2 → S2 0.87 becomes a loop of string; 86% S1 becomes a stack. The last stop is the empty spot where the S5 file should be. The camera pulls up, and the arrangement of everything on the desk *is* the S5 answer.

- **First 2 s:** close on the METR report's line about agents meant to be isolated, the camera already moving.
- **Beats:** report (hook) → wiki page (lens) → payload strip (method, abstentions) → three numbers land (results) → the empty S5 spot and the pull-up (S5) → two desks (channel vs model) → one line.
- **Look:** a top-down warm desk under one light, real documents as the textures.
- **Sound:** paper, a camera-dolly hum, your voice.

**Risk:** it sits close to the "evidence board" cliché, and it relies on document textures we'd have to make.
